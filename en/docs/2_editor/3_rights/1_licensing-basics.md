# Licensing: the essentials in three minutes

A track can be used if its open licence is no stricter than CC BY-NC or its author directly allowed it in the level. A track from YouTube, SoundCloud or Spotify needs its author's permission

> [!warning] Warning
> There is no official server or site for levels yet, they are in development. The rules below are already written. The Steam build already checks a level by them and publishes it to Steam Workshop - [[17_publishing]]

## In short

Suitable licences include, for example, `CC0`, `CC BY`, `MIT` or `SIL OFL 1.1`.
YouTube, SoundCloud and Spotify issue no licence to anyone, so a track from there can only be used with its author's permission

The same rule applies to images, fonts and any other files in a level.
More - [[2_legal-resource-paths]]

## Nothing is checked at home

A level on your disk is not checked. Not on create, not on save, not on load.
A level for yourself can be built from any files at all, the editor sets no conditions

Checking switches on at one moment: **when a level is handed to a service**

## The order of work

1. Pick a source before you build the level - [[3_where-to-get-resources]]
2. No suitable licence? Ask the author, also before you build - [[6_asking-permission]]
3. Fill in the resource's record as soon as you add the file - [[4_resource-record]]
4. Check the level before publishing - [[5_publish-readiness]]

## The level's licence

Levels are distributed under `CC BY-NC`:
- **CC** - Creative Commons
- **BY** - the author has to be credited
- **NC** - non-commercial use only

That licence covers **your work**: the placement, the timing, the keys, the composition.
It does not relicense other people's files inside the level. Every external resource keeps its own terms, and they are recorded separately, per resource

## Every resource has to qualify

A level qualifies only if every resource in it qualifies.
If even one fits neither of [[2_legal-resource-paths|the two options]], the level will not get onto the official server

## What the licence does not cover

The service's rules describe this:
- your statement that you hold the rights you claim
- the operator's right to moderate and remove levels
- the right to show a level's name in listings
- the disclaimer of liability

## Why non-commercial

I do not stand in the way of levels being shared freely.
The only thing I am against is commercial use

> [!info] Worth knowing
> The game is free: no purchases, no ads, no paid content, no donations tied to what is hosted. So the "non-commercial" condition is met for the platform itself. CC 4.0 licenses every recipient directly from the author, and the chain author - server - player needs no additional permission

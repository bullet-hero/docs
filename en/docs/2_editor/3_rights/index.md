# Rights

Licensing of music and art: what you may use, where to get it and how to record it

> [!warning] Warning
> The services this section talks about - the official server and the site - do not exist yet. They arrive at release. The rules below are already written and tested, but none of them has a button in the game yet

This section is for the moment a level leaves your disk. It explains which music, images and fonts a published level may contain, how to prove it, and how a service will grade a level before accepting it

## Nothing is checked at home

A level on your own device is graded by nothing at all: not on create, not on save, not on load. A level made for yourself out of any files is your own business. Everything in this section switches on at exactly one moment, when a level is offered to a service. The details are in [[1_licensing-basics]]

## Two layers of rights

| What | Terms |
|---|---|
| Your own work: layout, timing, keys, composition | `CC BY-NC` (attribution, non-commercial only) |
| Every external file inside the level | its own terms, recorded per resource |

The level's licence does not relicense the external files. A level qualifies only if every resource in it does, and a resource qualifies in exactly one of two ways described in [[2_legal-resource-paths]]:
- **an open licence** as free as `CC BY-NC` or freer, for example `CC0`, `CC BY`, `MIT` or `SIL OFL 1.1`
- **the rights holder's direct permission** that covers use in the level, free non-commercial redistribution by third parties and non-commercial use only

A permission turns a refused licence into a review by a person, never into a pass

## What the work looks like

1. Pick a source before you build. [[3_where-to-get-resources]] grades known sites, and YouTube, SoundCloud and Spotify are graded as not allowed, because they issue no licence to anyone
2. If a work has no suitable licence, ask its author before building a level around it. The letter template is in [[6_asking-permission]]
3. Fill in the resource's record at the moment you add the file, because that is the only moment you still remember where it came from. What goes into it is in [[4_resource-record]]
4. Before publishing, check the level against the verdicts described in [[5_publish-readiness]]: an error blocks the upload, a warning sends it to moderation, advice blocks nothing

An empty field anywhere in this model means "nothing was declared", never "everything is permitted". An unfilled licence is `NoSpecifiedLicense`, and that is refused

> [!info] Worth knowing
> The game is free and monetised in no way, so the non-commercial condition is satisfied for the platform itself. The developers do not stand in the way of levels being shared freely. What they are categorically against is commercial use
